Reception

One visit, from the desk to the room

  1. Admit the patient at reception

    Type a name, pick the priority and print a ticket with a queue number and a QR code.

  2. Call the next patient from the room

    Each room sees its own queue, most urgent first, and calls with one press of N.

  3. Show the call in the waiting room

    The TV shows the ticket and the room, reads it out, and never shows a name.

The patient follows their turn on their phone

The QR code on the ticket opens a page with their number, how many are ahead, the wait and the room. No app and no account.

When they are called, the page says so and tells them where to go. It closes once the visit ends.

Managers see where patients wait longest

Flags rooms over their wait target

Each room has a target. The longest wait is shown as one real case, not an average.

Lists rooms that need a decision now

A room with patients and nobody serving rises to the top before anyone has to ask.

Questions clinics ask

Does the patient need to install anything?

No. The QR code opens a web page in the phone's browser, and it works without an account.

What does the waiting-room screen need?

Any TV or monitor with a web browser. It opens the board's link and updates itself every 4 seconds.

How do staff sign in to QMan?

With the organisation code, a username and a password. New accounts set their own password the first time.

Who sees a patient's name?

Only staff whose role allows it. The board and the patient's page show ticket numbers only.

Set up rooms, routes and staff yourself

Rooms and routes

Staff accounts and roles

Four ready roles, or your own from 18 permissions, limited to the branches each person works in.

Patient names

Shown only to staff allowed to see them. Everyone else sees the ticket number.
